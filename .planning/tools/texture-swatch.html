Phase 4 — paper texture: which alpha?

Five panels below. One flat control, then the same 200×200 feTurbulence tile at four rect opacities. Everything else is identical: same ground #faf6ee, same 17px Literata body copy, same 30.6px Literata 600 heading.

What to look for, in this order:

  1. Is the grain visible at arm's length? Sit back from the screen. If a panel is indistinguishable from the flat control at normal reading distance, its alpha is too low to be worth any bytes at all.
  2. Is it invisible enough that it is never noticed as a pattern? Read the paragraph. If your eye leaves the words and goes to the background, the alpha is too high. "Barely perceptible fine even tooth" is the lock, not "visible paper".
  3. Are there seams on a 200px grid at 100% zoom? Look for faint horizontal or vertical lines every 200px. If you see them, the filter region is wrong, not the alpha — check that the tile carries x='0' y='0' width='100%' height='100%' on the <filter>. Do not fix a seam by lowering the alpha.
  4. Does the 17px Literata stay crisp over it? Look at the thin strokes and at the Vietnamese marks — the dot under ạ, the horn on ợ. Grain that muddies a diacritic costs more than it buys.

The first panel is 390px wide. CONTEXT locks the texture as identical on a 390px phone with no breakpoint and no reduced variant, so it has to be judged at that width too. Resize the window to 390px, or use device emulation, and re-run all four questions.

α 0.05 — at 390px (phone width) The mid candidate at the narrow width. Same tile, same alpha, no media query — this is what the phone gets.

Phạm Hà Khánh Chi

At 390px the ground is a narrow column and the grain has less area to establish itself in. Read this paragraph the way you would read a page on a phone held at arm's length: the texture should be there when you look for it and gone when you do not.

If the tile reads differently here than it does at desktop width, the alpha is wrong — there is no phone variant to fall back on, and none is going to be added.

Flat control — no texture The reference. Every judgement below is "compared with this".

A control, with nothing on it

This is the ground exactly as Phase 2 shipped it: #faf6ee, flat, no image. It is also the phase's named fallback — if the tile does not render faithfully on iOS Safari, one background-image declaration is deleted and the site looks like this.

Read the paragraph here first and form an impression of the page's weight. Then read the same paragraph on each textured panel. The question is never "is this nice"; it is "did the texture change what the type does".

α 0.035 — the quiet candidate rect opacity='0.035'. Expect to have to look for it.

A paper with almost no tooth

The lowest candidate. If this is indistinguishable from the control at arm's length, it is not worth shipping: the tile costs about 414 bytes and a decode, and a texture nobody can perceive buys nothing but a claim in a document.

The failure to watch for at this end is the opposite of the obvious one. Too little grain does not look bad — it looks like the flat control, which means the phase quietly did not happen.

α 0.05 — the research default rect opacity='0.05'. This is the value 04-RESEARCH prints in the token; it is a starting point, not a verdict.

A paper with a fine even tooth

The value the research listing carries. It is here as a candidate on exactly the same footing as the other three — the reason this page exists is that 0.05 was chosen from a description rather than from a screen, and a description cannot settle a perceptual question.

Note what the grain does to the 17px body copy specifically. Literata's strokes are finer than the old Roboto's at the same size, so the type has less mass to hold its own against a busy ground.

α 0.06 — one step up rect opacity='0.06'. The smallest step that is reliably distinguishable from 0.05.

A paper you can just about feel

If 0.05 reads as nearly-nothing on your display, this is the next stop. A 20% increase in alpha is a much smaller increase in perceived grain than the arithmetic suggests, which is why the candidates are not evenly spaced.

Watch the heading as well as the body copy. A 30.6px Literata 600 has enough mass that it will look fine long after the 17px paragraph has started to struggle — judge on the paragraph.

A paper that announces itself

The upper bound. This is included so the overshoot is visible rather than theoretical: PITFALLS calls register overshoot only partly recoverable, because a reader who has formed an impression of a busy page cannot un-form it.
